Getting Started

Chapter 1

The Basics

Based on Introduction · Section 1: Character Creation · Section 6: Types of Threads · Section 7: Battle Mechanics

The Game

Bleach: Online World (B:OW) is a forum roleplay set in the universe of Bleach. It is an Alternate Universe: none of the original characters from the anime or manga exist here. Players create their own characters, who live, travel, fight, and grow across four worlds: the Soul Society, Hueco Mundo, the Human World, and Hell.

B:OW is first and foremost a creative writing forum. Stats and numbers determine a lot, but the game is not about who has the best Stat and Skill build.

How Play Works

B:OW is played as Paragraph Roleplay (also called Forum Roleplay). Players write posts, made of paragraphs, inside threads. Each thread is a scene: a fight, an adventure, a training session, a social encounter, a job.

  • A character's actions are written by their player. There is no dice roll: outcomes follow from the characters' Stats, Skills, Class, and techniques, applied through the battle rules (see Battle).
  • When a thread ends, it is reported for grading. Staff review it and award rewards: Training Points, Ryo, and other gains depending on the thread type (see Threads).
  • Rewards are spent to raise Class, learn techniques, and increase Stats.

The Players

Every player may create multiple characters, but each character must have its own account on the forum. One account cannot hold several characters. Accounts can be linked through the same email.

  • An account is required to post anywhere except the Affiliates and Advertising and Free RP boards. Posts made without an account are deleted.
  • Do not create accounts or characters you don't intend to play.
  • Register your own account; do not ask another member to register it for you.
  • Account names may not be vulgar or offensive.

A character is defined by:

A character is created by posting an application ("app") using the race's template, which staff grade before the character can play (see Character Creation).

Staff grade applications, techniques, items, upgrades, and finished threads. Approvals are posted in the thread as 1/2 and 2/2. Senior Graders, experienced non-staff members, can grade standard threads and give advisory opinions on powers (see Threads). Staff also arbitrate conduct and disputes (see Conduct).

Chapter 2

Character Creation

Based on Section 1: Character Creation · Trial Apps · How to Start Strong and Start Easy

Overview

Creating a character takes six steps:

  1. Choose a race.
  2. Design the person: appearance and personality.
  3. Create a power set within your race's rules.
  4. Write the application with your race's template and submit it.
  5. Pick a starting point: Power Tier or Training Tier.
  6. Start writing once the character is approved.

No A.I. writing. B:OW is a creative writing forum, so using A.I. to write is strictly prohibited. Applications, posts, update submissions, and all other work must be entirely original. A member found using A.I. must rewrite that work in their own words. Failing to do so, or repeated use after warnings, can lead to: - rejected applications and other submissions - withheld thread rewards - zero legitimacy for character development in any thread where A.I. was used - demotion from organizational positions

A.I.-generated images are allowed. The forum is not responsible for, does not support, and is not associated with any monetization of A.I. art made by its members.

Step 1: Choose a Race

Race determines the type of power you have, the organizations you can join, your relationships with other characters, and the world you live in. All races and their Releases are described in Races.

Advice

Think carefully about what you want to do and who you want to be: a bloodthirsty Hollow preying on its victims, a righteous Vandenreich Quincy serving the Emperor, or one of the many ways of playing a Shinigami. Each race has advantages and disadvantages, and some builds are stronger than others, but this is a creative writing site. Pick a race because you want to create something entertaining, not because it is the "most powerful." First and foremost, you are here to have fun.

Step 2: Design the Person

Fill in the template's personal sections: appearance, then likes, dislikes, fears, goals, flaws, and overall personality.

Advice

For appearance, think about what makes the character noticeable and unique: hair, skin, accessories, clothing, birthmarks. Don't add scars liberally without explanation. For personality, keep asking "Why?" A character may be a loner because he fears large groups, because he was lost in a crowd as a child, and so he loses his cool in big groups. One reason leads to the next, and the application writes itself.

Step 3: Create a Power Set

Your race's entry in Races tells you what kinds of abilities your race can have, how many techniques you need to create, and any other requirements.

Advice

Powers are often the hardest section to write. The concept is usually easy; the execution and balance are hard. Many people start from a key word, direct ("fire," "healing") or vague ("suppression," "red"). Don't feel bad if you have trouble toning a power down to fit the system: staff and members are happy to help.

Step 4: Write and Submit the Application

All creations use the official templates, found in Index: Guides and Templates → Templates (also linked in the Helpful Links sidebar). Not every template is for characters.

Character applications

  1. Copy your race's template. Each section has an explanation beneath the template.
  2. In Create a Character, open the sub-board for that template.
  3. Start a new thread titled with the character's first and last name and paste the template.
  4. Fill in Password 1 and Password 2. These prove you have read the rules; both are found in the rules threads. If you can't find them, you will be sent back to read the rules.
  5. When every slot meets its requirements, staff move the application to Pre-Approved Characters. All that remains is assigning Stat Points (SP).

The SP you receive depends on the quality of the application. It is never less than 280 and never more than your race's SP cap (see Races).

Social characters. A character with no powers removes the Powers and Fighting Style sections, lists in Rank that it is a social character, and asks to be graded as a purely social application.

Techniques

Techniques use the Create a Technique template, posted in Create Customs. Fill it in with as much detail as possible.

  • Drawbacks are required and must be equal to the technique's strength. A technique that controls time would have a heavy drawback, such as organ dysfunction. A technique that guides projectiles would have little more than minor Reiatsu loss.
  • Once approved, a technique cannot be edited.

Reserved techniques

A technique can be reserved so that only your character can learn it.

Rule Limit
Reserved techniques per character 10 (Technique Master Type: unlimited)
Total techniques per character 20, reserved included. Training Tier: 23.
  • Teaching: you may teach a reserved technique to an ally in a Joint-Training thread, with the same paragraph count as normal training. You must have it Mastered to teach it, and the student can never Master it.
  • Grading: staff only grade a reserved technique if you have enough TTP to learn it.
  • Learning: once approved, you must learn it to at least Learned. You cannot create more reserved techniques until all your reserved techniques are at least Learned.

Organizations

Use Create Customs → Create an Organization. New threads there include a pre-filled template; fill it in and staff will review it. Organizations have fewer standards than other creations (see Ranks & Organizations).

Advice

It's up to you which path you take. If you can't decide, see How Applications Are Graded below.

How Applications Are Graded

From the Grading Guide "How to Start Strong and Start Easy" (thread 4629). It mainly applies to characters not starting in Training Tier, but not only to them. It describes how one grader grades; most other graders follow the same format.

The Class and Rank given on approval determine the SP awarded. The SP also depend on effort: an application with significantly more content receives more.

Uniqueness is one of the most valuable qualities. A highly unique application gets a "discount" on the effort expected in the other sections.

Starting Class Typical position What the application needs
7 Unseated, Unranked, Academy Student The bare minimum: only what the template's standards require.
6 Still Unranked or similar A bit more effort, but not much.
5 Low Rank: a low Seat, an exceedingly weak Numero More details and descriptive "fluff."
4 Fracción, Seated Officer, or similar Power sections filled out and explained entirely. A Shinigami receives Shikai; other races gain their powers too.
3 Vice Captain, strong Fracción, or comparable A significant amount of writing and detailed explanation; above the standard minimums.
2 Weak Captain, Low-Tier Espada, Privaron Espada, or similar Some sections may be shorter if balanced by significant effort elsewhere.
1 Captain, High-Tier Espada, Executive, or equivalent Particularly exceptional. Class 1 is the highest Class you can "app into," and it is hard to reach without uniqueness.

Complex powers

The more complex an ability, the longer it takes to grade, sometimes months, and the more likely the grader is to find problems. Common traits of complex abilities include, but are not limited to:

  • alternate resource mechanics
  • alternate forms of damage
  • needing multiple paragraphs to explain what the ability does
  • referencing or synergizing with techniques or abilities not listed in the profile
  • excessive terminology specific to the power
  • stacking synergies within a technique
  • Stat alterations
  • extremely steep drawbacks in exchange for extremely powerful effects

Having one or more of these does not mean the application will be denied.

Advice

If you're unsure how much to write, ask staff: they grade the applications and can show you examples that meet the criteria. To get graded quickly, avoid the complex traits listed above.

Step 6: Start Writing

Once approved:

  • Register the character in Who's Who? (thread 9655).
  • Put links to your Character Profile and Point Bank in your signature.
Advice

Your character's story keeps growing as you play. Join the Discord server, and above all, have fun.

Developing Your Character

History

Every template has a History section, and it is required. You can keep adding to a character's history over time.

Advice

Dramatic changes in a character are completely normal. Just add them to the character as they progress.

Change It Up (CIU)

A CIU is an update to a completed, accepted character. CIUs are graded and added to the application once approved. Post them with the CIU template in Upgrade Your Character, then report the thread for grading (see Threads).

Racial powers or other major changes Up to once a month A good In-Character reason and at least 3 threads supporting the change

New members who aren't ready to commit may create a Trial Character: a lighter application for testing the forum before writing a full one. Read Character Creation, Races, Types, and Battle first.

Chapter 3

Classes and Points

Based on Section 1: Character Creation

Overview

Threads reward Training Points (TP). TP is the raw currency of growth: on receiving it, you convert it into the points that actually improve your character.

From Into Ratio Used for
TP CTP (Character Training Points) 1 : 1 Raising Class
TP TTP (Technique Training Points) 1 : 1 Learning non-Release techniques
TP SP (Stat Points) 2 : 1 Stats (all races)
  • TP cannot be banked. It must be converted on receipt.
  • A reward may be split in any combination, as long as the parts add up to the TP awarded. For example, 1,000 TP can become 600 CTP + 400 TTP.
  • All points and changes are recorded in the character's Point Bank. There is no template for Point Banks, because each one is unique.
Advice

If you need help building a Point Bank once your character is accepted, ask staff or any member for examples.

Class

Class is B:OW's equivalent of level. It runs from Class 8, the bottom, to Class 0, the strongest.

Class determines:

  • Gravitational Pressure and Technique Clash (see Battle).
  • When new levels of your Release unlock, such as Bankai, Segunda Etapa, or Fullbring Level 2 (see Races).
  • Which non-Release techniques you may learn. You may learn techniques at most one Class above your own: a Class 3 character can learn up to Class 2. Some Types have exceptions.

Gaining Class

Convert TP into CTP (1:1). Accumulated CTP raises your Class. The CTP needed for each Class varies by race (see Races).

CTP comes from completed threads that award TP. Some thread types give much more because they are longer and harder:

  • Adventures and Quests: both the participants and the Dungeon Master are rewarded.
  • Player versus Player (PvP)
  • Solo Training and Joint Training threads

See Threads for all thread types.

Techniques and TTP

Non-Release techniques are found in the Technique Database. To learn one, spend TTP according to the technique's Class and meet its other requirements, such as race or Skills. For example, only Fullbringers can learn Bringer Light. Each technique lists its own requirements.

Record in your Point Bank the TTP spent, the technique, and its proficiency.

TTP cost per payment

Technique Class 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 1 0
TTP 5 10 15 20 40 80 180 250 600

Proficiency

Paying the cost more than once raises the technique's proficiency.

Technique of your Class or higher. Example: a Class 5 character learning a Class 4 technique, the highest Class it can learn, at 40 TTP per payment.

Proficiency Payments Total TTP Meaning
Learned 1 40 Knows the basic form and is grasping the concept. Must use incantations.
Proficient 2 80 Can perform it on the fly without much focus; may ignore incantations.
Mastered 3 120 Knows it inside and out; may develop new and creative ways to use it.

Technique of a lower Class than yours. The first payment makes it Proficient and the second Mastered. Example: a Class 5 character spends 15 TTP on a Class 6 technique and is Proficient; 30 TTP in total makes it Mastered.

  • You may pay the full amount at once, unless the technique says otherwise. A Class 5 character can spend 120 TTP to Master a Class 4 technique immediately, or 30 TTP to Master a Class 6 technique.
  • Class 0 techniques can only be used by Class 0 characters. Class 1 characters cannot use them.

Cinematic techniques

  • Cost half the usual TTP, rounded to the nearest 5.
  • Are classed by complexity, not power.
  • Do not count against the limit of 20 techniques.
Rank 1 2 3 4
Max. cinematic techniques 5 6 8 10

Stat Points (SP)

SP determine a character's Stats: physical, spiritual, and sensory capabilities (see Stats). The total available depends on race, Class, and Power Tier vs. Training Tier. SP can be spent on any Stat once the character is accepted and SP is awarded.

Per-Stat cap

No more than 400 SP may be spent on a single Stat, measured before Type boosts. Exceptions:

  • Kenpachi Type: adds a set amount of SP beyond the 400 cap.
  • Beast-Clansmen: up to 500 in physical and sensory Stats, at a cost to their other Stats (see Races).
  • High Rank: characters with a high enough Rank to gain extra SP from it may invest up to 500 in one Stat. That choice is locked in and cannot be changed.

Gaining SP

  1. Classing up: each Class gives SP; the amount varies by race (see Races).
  2. Converting TP: 2 TP = 1 SP, for all races. For example, 100 TP buys 50 SP.

Training cap. A character can gain at most 700 SP in total from training and conversion. Once you have converted 450 SP, only 250 more (500 TP) can be gained this way. After that, more SP only comes from raising Class. Some races have minor exceptions (see Races).

Skill Points (SkP)

Skills are rated from 1 to 4 (see Skills).

Rule Value
Starting SkP 15
Cost of a Skill at 4 5 SkP (one extra point)
SkP gained +1 per 8 completed threads, of any type. A DP or DE thread counts as two.
Maximum 25 SkP per character (Skilled Type: more)

Ryo (Finances)

Ryo is money. It gives access to the NPC, Custom Item, and Nobility systems.

Starting Ryo

Starting Rank 1 2 3 4
Ryo 5,000 15,000 30,000 50,000

Earning Ryo

  • Job threads: like Training threads, but they pay Ryo instead of TP, based on the paragraphs written.
  • Adventure and Quest threads: large Ryo rewards on completion.
  • Class 0 characters may take Ryo instead of TP as a reward.
  • Social threads: pay slowly, but allow more character development.

See Threads for amounts.

Races

Based on Section 2: Races, Releases, and Leveling

Overview

The setting has four worlds: the Soul Society, Hueco Mundo, the Human World, and Hell. Each race originates in one of them, but that does not limit where it can go. Any character may explore any world.

Each race entry gives:

  • Release: the race's signature power, and when its stages unlock.
  • Special rules unique to the race.
  • Leveling path: a table with these columns.
    • CTP: the total CTP needed to reach that Class.
    • SP: the SP gained on reaching it.
    • Total: the SP total without training.
  • SP caps: without training / with training (+700) / with training and Training Tier (+200 more).

Release durations are counted in posts. When a duration ends, the Release must be refreshed by paying its cost again, or it ends. Some Releases cannot be refreshed.

Soul Society Races

The Soul Society is the "heaven" of the Bleach universe. After death, souls may be sent there as Pluses.

  • Rukongai has 320 districts, 80 in each direction, numbered by distance from the center. District 1 is peaceful and lawful; District 80 is violent and crime-infested.
  • The Seireitei is a circular fortress with four gates, ten days' walk apart, each guarded by a Gatekeeper. It is home to the noble families, the Shinigami, and the Academy.

Playable races here are the Shinigami (including Rogues and Vizards) and the Beast-Clansmen.

Shinigami

Protectors of the spiritual world. Souls elevated by their spiritual power, who slay Hollows and protect souls to keep the cycle between the worlds stable.

Release: Zanpakutō

  • Sealed: a sword.
  • Released: something more powerful. It may not be a wieldable weapon, but it always has a physical form: an elemental or weaponized structure.
  • A Zanpakutō Release never changes the user's body.
Stage Unlocks Cost Duration
Shikai Automatically at Class 4 (counts as a Class 4 technique) 200 Reiatsu 10 posts, then pay again or it reverts to sealed
Bankai By application, Class 3 minimum 800 Reiatsu (at Class 3) 10 posts

Costs and durations improve with Class (see the leveling path).

Bankai

Who may use it. Only Vizards and characters Ranked Third Seat or above. Slots are limited:

  • At most two Third Seats and four Vice Captains may have Bankai at a time.
  • Captains and Vizards always have Bankai.
  • An occupied slot may be contested. In that fight, the holder may use Bankai and the challenger may not.
  • To start with Bankai at character creation, you must apply for a Captain position. Any other Rank goes through the normal Bankai application.

Bankai application (from Class 3, using the form in Upgrade Your Character):

  1. Three threads with significant, in-depth character development pushing toward Bankai, with generous evidence in every thread that it is a serious goal. If the character is not yet of the proper Rank, these threads also count toward promotion.
  2. Then, depending on the slot:
    • Slot free: a solo thread of significant development with the Zanpakutō spirit, training together. Minimum one page, each post at least four paragraphs.
    • Slot occupied: a qualifying PvP that the challenger must win, fought without Bankai against an opponent who may use it. It must include in-depth development with the Zanpakutō spirit, fighting together against a common "enemy."

The moderator grading the application makes the final decision. Available slots are listed in The Soul Society Rankings.

Special rules

  • All Shinigami start with Shunpo at Proficient. Mastery must still be trained.

Rogues

Rogue Shinigami wield Bakkōtō, illegal weapons made by the Kasumi'Oji family.

Release: Bakkōtō

  • No weapon-type restriction. It can be anything.
  • Usable as soon as the character reaches Class 7. No training is needed, unlike a Zanpakutō.
  • Has one ability and no techniques. The ability grows in levels with Class, reaching several levels by Class 1.
  • Every level costs 200 Reiatsu.
  • The user may always release at a lower level to trade power for duration.
Bakkōtō level 7, 6, 5 4, 3 2, 1
Duration 7 posts 5 posts 3 posts

Life Force

Rogues have an extra Stat, Life Force (LF).

  • 10 LF per point of Spiritual Pressure.
  • LF can be converted 1:1 into Reiatsu Strength to strengthen the Bakkōtō. The conversion lasts for the whole Release, even when switching levels.
  • Powering the Bakkōtō does not drain LF by itself. Every hit the Rogue takes costs 100 LF.

Stats

Based on Section 3: Stats & Skills

Overview

Stats give numerical evidence of a character's abilities. There are nine, bought with SP, plus two Auras that are free.

Group Stats
Physical Strength, Speed
Spiritual Reiatsu Strength (RS), Spiritual Pressure (SpPr)
Sensory Instinct, Senses, Energy Sensory (ES)
Miscellaneous Healing Expertise (HE), Light Aura, Dark Aura

Distribution rules

  • Divide SP however you like, but your Stats must add up exactly to your SP cap before Type advantages are applied. After Type advantages, the total may exceed the cap.
  • No single Stat may receive more than 400 SP. The exceptions are Beast-Clansmen (see Races) and the Kenpachi Type, which has its own distribution rules (see Types).
  • Stat values are not units of measurement and do not scale linearly: 150 Strength is not three times stronger than 50.

What a gap means

Having a higher Stat than your opponent only makes a major difference with a significant gap, about 100–150 points. There are still ways for the "weaker" character to overpower the "stronger" one.

The three-times cap

No Stat can be boosted beyond three times its value after Type, by any boost or technique. A Strength of 800 after Type can reach 2,400 at most. Exceptions are noted where they apply, such as step techniques for Speed.

Offensive Stats

Three Stats are offensive: Strength, Reiatsu Strength, and Spiritual Pressure. Your dominant offensive Stat, the highest of the three, generally becomes your attack strength. Each Stat's entry says what it powers.

Offensive Stat Powers
Strength Anything physical, including physically based Release forms. If it is your highest offensive Stat, it is your offense against defenses like Hierro and Blut and when your weapon's physical form clashes with an enemy's. Not used if the weapon has a non-physical form, such as gas.
Reiatsu Strength All non-Release techniques (Kidō, Cero, Hierro, Blut), offensive and defensive. If it is your highest offensive Stat, it is also your attack strength, offensive power, and the durability of your Release weapon. Decides clashes between similar techniques.
Spiritual Pressure Only the Release, Release weapons, and a Mod Soul's unique abilities. Never Reiatsu techniques or physical combat. It sets the Release's attack power if it is your highest offensive Stat, unless the Release is physical and Strength is your highest.

Weapons

A weapon's durability and cutting power are based on your highest offensive Stat. With at least twice the opponent weapon's offensive Stat, you break through it in one clean hit. Repeated trauma can still destroy a spirit weapon without that advantage.

RS ↔ SpPr conversion

  • Spend 300 Reiatsu, standing still and focusing, to move points between Spiritual Pressure and Reiatsu Strength.
  • Example: with 400 in both, moving 100 gives 300 SpPr and 500 RS.
  • Only once every three posts.

Overview

Skills are not techniques or Release powers. They are specialties the character can use in everyday life or in battle: studying, picking locks, mastery of a weapon.

Anyone can try anything at a basic, non-Skill level, such as swinging a sword or studying. To be especially good at something, a character needs points in the matching Skill.

3 Mastered 3 SkP Class 2 An expert, almost unrivaled.
4 Special Mastery 5 SkP Class 1 or Rank 3 Beyond expert; accomplishes the seemingly impossible. A lower Class can reach it at Rank 3.

Skill Points

  • Every character starts with 15 SkP.
  • +1 SkP per 8 completed NDE threads. A DP or DE thread counts as two.
  • At most 10 extra, for 25 SkP in total. The Skilled Type has a higher maximum (see Types).
  • Levels 1–3 cost one point each. Going from 3 to 4 costs two, so a Skill at 4 costs 5 SkP in total.
  • SkP can be banked for later.
  • At most two Skills at level 4 at a time, regardless of Rank. The Skilled Type may have more if it has the SkP.

Skills back up Fighting Style

A character cannot use anything in their Fighting Style without the Skills to back it up. A complicated sword style needs SkP in a Swords Skill. The same applies to individual techniques and to defenses against other races' techniques. After pre-approval, staff will tell you if your SkP is insufficient for your Fighting Style.

Cinematic and social Skills

Skills with no battle influence (singing, dancing, writing, art, and the like) need no SkP. They don't have to be listed in the Skills section and can stay purely In-Character.

Each category allows custom "Other" Skills. Include them during pre-approval, with a note under the Skills list explaining what they do.

Types

Based on Section 4: Types

Overview

A Type is a character's personality archetype: the kind of person they are. A Type alters Stats, weakening less important ones to strengthen more relevant ones. Its effects grow with the character's Rank, from Rank 1 (weakest benefits) to Rank 4 (strongest).

Terms used in this chapter:

  • Physical Stats: Strength and Speed.
  • Spiritual Stats: Reiatsu Strength and Spiritual Pressure.
  • Sensory Stats: Senses, Instinct, and Energy Sensory.
  • "+50%" means the Stat is multiplied by 1.5 (100 → 150). "×2" doubles it (100 → 200). "−25%" multiplies it by 0.75 (100 → 75).
  • Percentages apply to the Stat as assigned (a Non-Typed baseline).
  • Free Skill points granted by a Type don't count toward the SkP total and cannot raise a Skill above its normal cap.

Changing Type

A Type change needs a strong In-Character reason and at least three threads showing the character developing toward the new Type. They can be any kind of thread, as long as they show the IC change. Non-Typed characters follow extra rules (see Non-Typed).

Chapter 8

Ranks and Organizations

Based on Section 5: Rank Requirements & Organizations

Overview

Ranks are positions of authority within organizations. Some are prerequisites for higher tiers of Release; others give command over subordinates. Every Rank also maps to a Type Rank (1–4), which sets the strength of the character's Type (see Types).

Getting a Rank

  • Open position: apply for it in the character application, or later with a CIU. A CIU promotion needs at least three threads justifying it. Any thread type counts, including a lost PvP, as long as it shows the character's desire to improve and grow.
  • Taken position: Ranks can be stolen in combat. If you lose a Rank Battle, the challenger takes your Rank and you drop to the next available one. This can cause a chain of battles if the positions beneath you are taken.

Leader bonuses

Some positions give a boost to all Stats after Type. These are marked +25% or +50% below.

Current holders of each position are listed in the forum's Rankings threads (Soul Society, Hueco Mundo, Human World, Vandenreich, Mercenary).

Thread Titles

The title carries the thread's details: a name, and optionally a difficulty, threat level, rating, and other tags. Example: "The Fullbringers: Whirlwind of Death [R, NDE, Extreme]." Not every detail is required, but it's wise to include as many as possible.

Threat Level

Every thread has a threat level, including Socials, which are typically NDE. It sets how permanently dangerous the thread is.

Level Injuries Death
NDE (Non-Death Enabled) Don't carry over after the thread. You may voluntarily keep injuries that fit your character's canon. None. A death can be hand-waved as never having happened; the character can simply be severely injured or defeated.
DP (Damage Permanent) Must carry over to later threads until healed at the hospital thread or In-Character. Lost limbs, scars, damaged organs. None.
DE (Death Enabled) Permanent. Permanent and irreversible.
Advice

DE is highly challenging and not recommended for beginners. Dungeon Masters generally won't kill you off on a whim, for the sake of the story, but they won't save you from your own mistakes either. DP threads let you feel real threat and grow from your injuries without risking death.

Rating

The rating sets how graphic and violent the content can be. Higher ratings such as "R" may include strongly sexual or graphically violent themes: foul language, blood, gore.

  • Overly sexual or violent content must go in a spoiler or fade to black.

  • Staff handle rating violations. There is room for interpretation, but obvious and blatant disregard is dealt with immediately and can lead to punishment.

Battle Threads

Any fight between characters outside Adventures and Canons, often to prove who is better or to further a goal. Battles are one of the most popular thread types and the primary source of TP. Rewards are based on posts and paragraphs, not pages, and vary by threat level.

Player versus Player (PvP)

A PvP is a fight between your character and another character on the site. Teams are allowed and still count as PvP.

Setting up a PvP

  • Outline the basic conditions with the thread template, edited as needed.
  • The location is the board you post in. To fight in Karakura Town, post in General Karakura: a thread made in the Soul Society can't be a fight in Karakura Town. Threads in the Battle Zone, which has no location of its own, must state their location.
  • Each participant writes an Introductory Post: the character's entrance, in any context. Attacks may be made in it, but nothing complex.
  • List all your Stats and Skills in spoilers at the end of the Introductory Post.

DE PvPs

NDE and DP fights are always available. A DE PvP must meet all three conditions below. If any is missing, staff grade it as NDE.

  1. Plot relevance. More than a normal meeting: lasting effects on the characters' plots, outcomes, and psyche. Fights between members of the same race are usually not allowed as DE, because many organizations forbid killing within them, so most such fights count as training. Exceptions depend on the circumstances and the organization.
  2. Actual threat of death. Spars don't count, even plot-relevant ones; usually only enemies qualify. Two Shinigami qualify only if they truly intend to kill each other. Having the potential to kill isn't enough: the intention or motivation must be there.
  3. Acknowledgement of the possibility of death. No assumptions. By joining, you agree to let the other player kill your character if the circumstances permit.

Out of Combat Threads

These threads aren't combat-related, so they're free from restrictions such as Reiatsu costs. They are simply ways for characters to interact.

Social Threads

Social threads cover talking, building relationships, chance encounters, parties, and the like. They have few rewards, but give room for character development: relating to others, changing personality, finding new goals.

  • A completed Social counts as one thread toward your next SkP.
  • Open threads can be entered by any player, regardless of affiliation, to join or ruin the event. Closed threads are limited to specific characters. Tag the title [Open] or [Closed].

Social bonus

Relationships, positive or negative, push characters to grow, and the Social bonus models that.

  • For every 10 paragraphs you write in a Social, you earn a one-time +10% bonus on the TP of the next thread you do with the same character, up to +50% for 50 paragraphs.
  • Each partner gives a separate bonus. Bonuses don't stack: in a thread with two of those partners, use one bonus and bank the other.
  • Each Social builds its own bonus. A second Social doesn't top up the first.
  • Record bonuses in your Point Bank. They apply to any thread that awards TP (Adventure, PvP, Training), or to the Ryo of a Job thread.
  • Bonuses belong to the character who earned them and can't be swapped to another of your characters.
  • Partner leaves the site or retires: the remaining participant may use the bonus in a thread with a different character. State this when reporting the thread, so staff can verify.

Example: A writes 50 paragraphs in a Social with B and C, and gets +50% for the next thread with B and +50% for the next with C. B wrote only 35 paragraphs, so B gets +30% with each of the others. If A and B later do a second Social with 20 paragraphs each, A holds +50% and +20%, and B holds +30% and +20%; the bonuses don't merge.

Battle

Based on Section 7: Battle Mechanics

Overview

Fights work much like the Bleach canon, and many attacks and abilities are the same or nearly the same as in the show. A fight is an exchange of posts. Stats, Class, Skills, and techniques decide what is possible, and the rules in this chapter decide how they interact.

Advice

Battling isn't as complicated as it sounds, and a helpful member base will guide you and answer questions. If you've never seen the show, there is a learning curve; that's what these rule sections are for.

How a Fight Works

  1. Attack: state an attack in your post.
  2. Defend: in the next post, your opponent may defend. If the defense fails, the attack hits.
  3. Multiple defenses: a target may have several layers of defense. Example: your attack breaks their weaker barrier, but in their next post they reveal an active Hierro stronger than your attack, and the second defense holds.
  4. Changed circumstances: if circumstances change, the defender gets another chance. If their sword breaks while blocking and countering, they may slightly alter their actions in the next post so they make sense and allow a secondary defense. Slightly alter means slightly alter, not change completely.

Auto-hit

An attack can have a 100% chance of hitting, with no chance of defense, only if no defense is plausible. Example: the target is immobile, drained of Reiatsu, and has no ally to help.

Stamina

Long, drawn-out fights tire characters. Constant exertion at high speed and power is taxing, even for characters who outlast ordinary people.

  • Show exertion: characters must slow down, stop, pant, catch their breath. If they don't, the opponent may claim that exhaustion prevents certain actions, and depending on the circumstances that can be a plausible complaint.
  • Reiatsu depletion drains stamina. Higher-Class techniques are more exhausting. Using technique after technique without fatigue isn't allowed; a character who does would be unable to move by the end.
  • Players must track their own character's stamina and keep it plausible. Stamina is an interpretation, not a Stat. If a player neglects it, it can be used against them.
Advice

It's smart to keep your character's stamina in mind.

End-of-post records

At the end of every post, list whatever is relevant:

  • Stat changes to your character or your opponent's.
  • Reiatsu used or regained, and the amount remaining.
  • (Preferred) descriptions of the techniques used, in spoilers, so others can read them without hunting them down.

Hidden moves are illegal

  • You can't dock Reiatsu now and use a technique later. A hidden move doesn't count.
  • When you spend Reiatsu, state in the post what it was used for, how much, and why.
  • Moves can be hidden In-Character. Hidden mines, for example, are allowed, but you must write where they are in that post. The opponent then plays as if their character doesn't know, unless they have a way to sense them.
  • Reiatsu is docked on activation. Charging doesn't dock Reiatsu.

Disputes

If there is a dispute, go to a staff member. They decide the outcome after hearing the opinions of other members, like a jury.

Battle Politeness

This is a rule.

  • Don't nitpick. Call out genuinely unreasonable details, like bending an arm into bone-breaking positions out of nowhere. Don't argue over trifles, like bending back one more inch or ducking during Shunpo. Give opponents the benefit of the doubt.

  • Charge-up techniques don't advance during cinematics. Seals that need a number of posts to activate don't count cinematic posts, so a battle can't be stalled for boosts.

Advice

Be polite: the thread is more enjoyable if you don't attack each other over minor details.

Cutback Effect

When you hit someone whose Spiritual Pressure exceeds yours by 150 or more, their body pushes back and you take the damage. This applies to all races except Arrancar, who use Reiatsu Strength instead (see below).

What triggers it:

  • All melee attacks: axes, Zanpakutō, punches, kicks, and the like.
  • Not special or Reiatsu-based attacks, such as a Reiatsu-charged blade or a Cero.
  • Releases negate Cutback when you hit with any part of your Release.

Getting around it:

  • Sacrifice Reiatsu Strength into Spiritual Pressure to increase cutting ability. Stand still and charge (you can't charge while moving), and state the amount in an OOC paragraph at the end of the post.
  • Arrancar: Reiatsu Strength sets their cutting power, and Cutback doesn't occur if their RS is within the right numbers.
  • High Strength: even when suffering Cutback, a character with significantly high Strength still deals blunt trauma and internal damage and can send the opponent flying or skidding.

  • Uses base points only, not points from Type boosts.
  • Raising SpPr raises maximum Reiatsu, but not current Reiatsu. Going from 200 to 300 SpPr turns 1,600/2,000 into 1,600/3,000.
  • Lowering SpPr can cost excess Reiatsu. Going from 300 to 200 turns 2,200/3,000 into 2,000/2,000.

    • Power Tier: the History section must contain a good portion of the family's history.

Chapter 14

Conduct, Posting, and Player Rules

Based on Rules of Conduct · Out-of-Character Rules for Players · Rules for Posting

Overview

This chapter covers how members treat each other, how posts are written, and the out-of-character limits that apply to every player.

"Out-of-Character" (OOC) means anything not voiced through a character: threads, tweets, chat messages, and so on with nothing to do with your In-Character (IC) context. Heavily disobeying these rules may lead to punishment.

Rules of Conduct

1. Zero Tolerance Policy

No overt or intended discrimination or harassment toward anyone, based on gender, age, sexuality, or any other status that could be used as a basis for ill treatment.

  • Accidental slips: unintended meanings or a bad take that could be read this way get a warning and a reminder that it isn't tolerated.
  • Reports: every report of someone being made to feel discriminated against, or uncomfortable through harassment, is taken very seriously and investigated. Staff hear all parties.
  • Intent or repetition: with sufficient proof of intent, or repeated behavior despite warnings, staff act decisively to prevent further harm.

Staff DMs are open for concerns.

2. Be respectful

  • Be kind. No offensive language or racial slurs.
  • Get to know new people, and what humor and teasing they like, before making questionable jokes.
  • If something offends you or makes you uncomfortable: keep a level head, take screenshots or save logs, submit them to staff, avoid engaging further, and let staff handle it.

3. Remember it's a game

B:OW is a roleplay forum, meant to be an escape from reality.

  • Disagreeing with a threadmate: stay calm and talk to them first. For help with a ruling, approach a staff member.
  • Don't pressure others about posts or gradings. Real people with real lives are playing, and no one knows everything others are going through. Be patient.

4. The Platinum Rule

Treat others the way they wish to be treated.

  • Let others have a voice. Disagreeing doesn't make someone wrong, or you right.
  • If someone is visibly upset, you have gone too far. Apologies are appreciated, but be willing to talk things out.
  • If it escalates, involve staff.

5. Play in good faith

  • Don't exploit loopholes or disrupt roleplay in any way.
  • The rules exist for a reason, and a lot of thought went into the mechanics, even if they differ from how you'd personally like to play.
  • Staff welcome suggestions. If yours is rejected, don't take it personally: staff will explain why and ask for understanding in return.
Advice

Different people find different things funny, and that's okay: let people enjoy what makes them laugh. Remember to have fun.

Rules of Engagement

1. Arguments not to use when discussing balance

Gradings can be subjective. Staff try to keep things level with the current site meta, but powers won't always pass exactly as designed. Don't argue from:

  • Outliers in Bleach. Canon sometimes shows things that could never be balanced on the site, such as Ishida firing 1,200 arrows consecutively, or characters doing something far beyond anything they ever did before and never repeating it. Some canon examples are valid, so choose carefully.
  • Old gradings. Balance on the site is in flux, and balance has an expiration date. Don't justify a strong or broken character with a grading made long ago or by another staff member. Staff may deliberately shift the meta; it's never personal.
  • Legacy or cinematic characters. Some semi-active characters still have ludicrously powerful abilities. Their existence doesn't entitle your character to that power. Don't compare yourself to characters made 2+ years ago. Any character can be rebalanced if someone complains or if the player feels it's needed.

2. What defines a rule

A rule is a decision made by staff on any grading.

  • Submissions almost always go through a dual approval process, so multiple staff members see them.
  • For big additions, such as new Types, races, or custom organizations, and sometimes a single character's power, all staff may weigh in, at their discretion.
  • This can make gradings take longer. Please be patient.

3. When to ask a mod, and how to treat rulings

  • Talk to your threadmates first. If anything in a thread is unclear or shaky, discuss it with them before going to staff. It's more respectful, gives them a chance to be heard, and often turns out to be a simple misunderstanding. If talking doesn't help, staff intervention is absolutely encouraged.
  • You may contest rulings. Staff are human and make mistakes, and a good argument can change their mind. But if a staff member stands firm, respect the decision: it's up to them to explain their reasoning. Don't fight with them; step away from the keyboard if you're heated. Not every submission will be approved as written.

Rules for Posting

These apply to all thread types.

  1. Third person and posting order. Posts narrate your character's actions in a third-person story format. Before starting, participants agree on a Posting Order. Posting Out Of Turn (OOT) gets you disqualified from the thread.
  2. Paragraphs and post length.
    • A paragraph is 5–12 sentences, or at least four lines in the post. Shorter paragraphs don't count, or are combined with another short paragraph when grading.
    • Every post needs at least two paragraphs, with no exceptions. Shorter posts are ignored in grading. Beyond that, write as much as is appropriate.
  3. OOC and IC. Label out-of-character remarks in a post "OOC", and label the return to your character "IC". You can also separate the two parts, as long as the separation is extremely clear.
  4. Language. Complete sentences and proper spelling and grammar, to the best of your ability. A post others can't understand may have to be fixed immediately.
  5. Colors.
    • Narration (actions, fighting) stays in the default color.
    • Dialogue and thoughts each get their own color; for example, dialogue in red and thoughts in green.
    • Thoughts are italicized, while dialogue goes in quotation marks.
    • When you control several characters in one thread, each needs different dialogue colors and different thought colors.
  6. Records.
    • Your introduction post ends with your Stats and Skills in a spoiler.
    • Any post where you used Reiatsu ends with your remaining Reiatsu.
    • If your Stats changed during the thread, add a new spoiler with the new numbers, such as your new Speed after being slowed.
    • At the end of a post, list in a spoiler the techniques used and their Reiatsu cost.

  7. Rule of Skip. A thread moving too slowly may skip someone who can't post:

    • with consent from that player and the thread leader (DM, GM, or otherwise), or
    • automatically, if the agreed time between posts has passed.

    The skipped player posts again on their next turn. This also applies to thread leaders, if their post isn't essential to the thread.

Trial Applications define a paragraph as 6–9 sentences or four lines; these posting rules say 5–12 sentences or four lines.

Rules for Players

Submission limits

  • Concurrent requests: no more than three grading requests at a time for anything that isn't a thread. This is per player, not per character. Anything beyond three is ignored, and you'll be asked to resubmit once a slot opens. Regular threads (PvP, Joint Training, Adventure, etc.) are exempt.
  • Batch size: each technique or item submission can contain at most five techniques or five items. A series of 15 must be split into three submissions, which uses up all three request slots.

Character slots

Every player starts with three character slots. To open more:

Character Requirement to open a slot

  • There is no total limit on slots.
  • TP from grading doesn't count toward opening slots.
  • Losing slots: if a character retires or dies, its slot is lost. With six slots, losing the sixth character brings you to five, and the CTP (or TTP) must be earned again to unlock a new one.
  • Buying slots: if all your characters have already opened slots and you can't earn more the normal way, you can buy up to two a year: 100,000 Ryo for the first and 200,000 Ryo for the second. The year starts at the first purchase, not on the calendar, and resets whether or not both were bought.
  • Returning characters: bringing back a retired character needs an available slot. Its original slot can't be reused.

Race changes (not from Saigo no Houmen)

When a character changes race:

  • Lost: all banked TTP and Ryo, set to zero.
  • Kept: CTP, trained SP, SkP, and any Training Tier benefits.
  • Techniques: those the new race can use are kept. All others are refunded at 75% of their TTP, without needing an Amnesia Pill.
  • Optionally, a full reset to Class 8.

Section 2's "Racial Changes" lists different losses for race changes such as conversion, Redemption, and Mask On: training SP and banked TTP are lost, Ryo and SkP kept, and techniques lost without refund (see Races). The two sections disagree, so check with staff.

Character death

  • Deaths are permanent, unless the death relates to a race change.
  • Revival requires a valid In-Character reason. The player presents it to staff, who decide whether the character can be revived and plan how.
  • A dead character loses all progress: CTP, TTP, SP, SkP, Ryo, techniques, everything. Progress is kept only after a race change or an approved revival.
